DAVID EXPLORA · KILIMANJARO

Prepare for Kilimanjaro beyond the summit photograph

Good preparation makes the whole journey easier to understand. It begins before the flight, continues at the equipment check and includes honest conversations on the trail. This is a planning guide; individual health decisions belong with your clinician and mountain guide.

David Explora planning notes · 13 October 2026 · 2 min read

Give arrival day a purpose

Arrange your airport pickup and a hotel night before the trek. A late flight followed immediately by a gate transfer leaves little time to rest, find missing equipment or discuss the route. Share flight details before your final itinerary is issued.

Keep your passport, quoted itinerary, insurance information and contact numbers accessible. Ask which equipment must be in your hand luggage in case checked bags are delayed.

Check the equipment you will actually use

Review boots, rain protection, warm layers, sleeping equipment and a headlamp with your crew. Confirm what the operator supplies, what is rented and what is personal. Try your boots and pack before the trip rather than discovering a poor fit on the mountain.

Discuss food requirements, tent or hut arrangements and the items you need close to you each day. The useful packing list is the one matched to your route and season.

Fitness and altitude are different questions

The CDC explains that physical fitness does not prevent altitude illness. Gradual ascent matters, and symptoms should be reported rather than hidden. Do not keep ascending to a higher sleeping altitude while experiencing altitude-illness symptoms.

Before travel, discuss relevant health history and any medication questions with a qualified clinician. On the mountain, tell your guide promptly if you feel unwell. Worsening symptoms need an immediate assessment and the guide’s response; this article is not a treatment plan.

Understand the safety plan before departure

Success includes a well-managed return. No advertised success rate can replace preparation, a suitable itinerary and a crew that can make careful decisions.

  • Ask who makes turnaround decisions and how symptoms are monitored.
  • Confirm the quoted emergency communication, evacuation and rescue arrangements.
  • Check that your insurance covers your planned trekking activity and altitude.
  • Leave time after descent before adding a long transfer or another demanding activity.
