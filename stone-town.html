WHY STONE TOWN?

More than an old town.

Stone Town is the historic heart of Zanzibar — a place where Swahili life, Indian Ocean trade, architecture and everyday coastal culture meet within a living urban landscape.

UNESCO recognizes Stone Town as an outstanding example of a Swahili coastal trading town. Its streets, buildings and architectural details reflect more than a millennium of interaction between African, Arab, Indian and European cultures.

That history is still visible when you walk through the town: narrow streets, carved wooden doors, old courtyards, historic buildings, small shops and waterfront spaces all form part of the experience.

This is why we don't see Stone Town simply as a list of monuments to visit. We see it as a place to slow down, look closer and understand another side of Zanzibar.

A town shaped by the Indian Ocean.

HISTORY THAT MATTERS

Understand the difficult chapters too.

Stone Town's history is not only a story of architecture, trade and cultural exchange. It is also connected to the history of the East African slave trade and its eventual suppression.

Christ Church Anglican Cathedral stands on the site of Zanzibar's last open slave market and commemorates the abolition of the slave trade. This difficult history is part of understanding Stone Town and the people who lived through it.

At David Explora, we believe responsible travel should make space for these histories with respect, context and honesty — not present Zanzibar only as a beautiful island destination.

RESPONSIBLE TRAVEL

Explore a living heritage site with respect.

Stone Town is both a World Heritage site and a living commercial and socio-cultural centre. UNESCO notes that the historic town faces pressures including development, traffic and tourism.

When visiting, respect residents, local customs, religious spaces and historic buildings. Follow local guidance and remember that Stone Town is someone's home and workplace as well as a place visitors come to discover.
